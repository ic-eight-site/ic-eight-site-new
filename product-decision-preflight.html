Product Decision · Preflight

Before you test the product, define what would count.

Product Decision Preflight starts with what a company publicly promises, then turns that promise into a focused question for actual use. It keeps the claim, the verification hypothesis, and what remains unknown visibly separate.

Saved, static examples. Viewing this page does not retrieve a website or call a model or API. These are public-information preflights, not completed Product Decision Reviews.

How to read the sample

A promise is a starting point. The test still has to be designed.

Each example preserves the evidence chain from an official public page to a question that can later be answered through controlled product use. Nothing in the preflight is presented as an observed product result.

Company claim

What the product says

A paraphrase and the exact public wording that supports it.

Verification hypothesis

What actual use should test

A user goal, first meaningful success, and observable check.

Still unknown

What the pages cannot prove

Quality, reliability, fit, and outcomes that require product evidence.

Napkin

Two distinct public promises lead to two different actual-use questions: one about turning text into a usable visual, and one about building a reviewable presentation deck.

Official host: napkin.ai

Company promise · paraphrase

Napkin promises to turn written ideas into editable visuals intended to make them easier to understand and share.

See the exact public evidence

“Napkin generates the most relevant visuals based on your text”

Source E8 · napkin.ai ↗

Source E10 · napkin.ai ↗

“Napkin generates visuals that make your ideas easier to understand and share.”

Source E41 · napkin.ai ↗

“you pick the one that best expresses what you have in mind.”

Feature source E8 · napkin.ai ↗

“Export them as .ppt, .png, .pdf or .svg”

Feature source E15 · napkin.ai ↗

Verification hypothesis · not yet tested

Can pasted text produce a visual that expresses the intended idea well enough to refine and share?

Proposed user goal
Turn existing text into a visual that can be refined and used in shared content.
First meaningful success
After importing a short passage, a relevant visual is visible, one option can be edited, and the result can be exported for inspection.

What actual use would check

  1. Import a short passage containing a process or related ideas.
  2. Compare the generated options with the source text.
  3. Edit one visual and confirm the change is retained.
  4. Export it as PNG or PDF and inspect the file.

Company promise · paraphrase

Napkin promises that its Slides offering can turn ideas into complete, ready-to-present decks through a collaborative pre-build process.

See the exact public evidence

“a visual communication platform that helps you turn ideas into visuals and presentations.”

Source E40 · napkin.ai ↗

“Napkin Slides turns your ideas into complete, ready-to-present decks.”

Source E42 · napkin.ai ↗

“It collaborates with you before building anything”

Source E42 · napkin.ai ↗

Verification hypothesis · not yet tested

Does Napkin Slides collaborate on an idea outline before producing a draft deck that the user can review as their own?

Proposed user goal
Develop ideas into a complete presentation deck that feels aligned with the user's intent.
First meaningful success
A short outline produces a visible draft deck after a collaboration step, with slides available for review.

What actual use would check

  1. Provide an outline with a topic and several key points.
  2. Proceed through the collaboration or clarification stage.
  3. Check whether a complete draft reflects the supplied points.
  4. Confirm whether the deck can be edited or presented.

Still unknown

What public pages cannot establish

  • Generation quality, relevance, reliability, speed, and editing behavior in actual use.
  • Whether audiences find the visuals easier to understand, effective, or memorable.
  • Plan limits, full input support, export fidelity, collaboration details, and complete feature scope.
  • Whether nuanced content, brand requirements, accessibility needs, and real publishing workflows are preserved.

Sources and evidence boundary

The preflight used bounded HTML and text from the submitted official host. It did not inspect JavaScript, images, PDFs, logins, or the product itself. Twenty-five of 103 captured passages were supplied to the saved analysis; the full website was not examined.

Parseur

The public promise is document automation. The verification questions separate successful extraction and delivery from broader claims about fewer errors or sustained operational value.

Information capturedOctober 11, 2026 · 14:03 UTC
Official host: parseur.com

Company promise · paraphrase

Parseur promises to convert PDFs, scans, emails, and images into structured data and deliver that data to existing applications, spreadsheets, and databases.

See the exact public evidence

“turns PDFs, scans, emails and images into accurate, structured data”

Source E5 · parseur.com ↗

“delivers it to the apps, spreadsheets and databases your team already uses”

Source E5 · parseur.com ↗

“Instantly send extracted data to all your applications.”

Source E1 · parseur.com ↗

“Drag and drop through the app, forward them to a dedicated email address, push them in via API”

Feature source E27 · parseur.com ↗

“connect to integrations like Zapier, Make, or n8n.”

Feature source E27 · parseur.com ↗

“Set up extraction, monitor workflows, and manage operations from a simple interface.”

Feature source E62 · parseur.com ↗

Verification hypothesis · not yet tested

Can the needed fields be extracted from a representative document and delivered to the chosen destination?

Proposed user goal
Turn incoming documents into structured data and send it to an existing work tool without manual re-entry.
First meaningful success
A sample invoice or email produces the required structured fields in a selected spreadsheet, database, or application.

What actual use would check

  1. Create a test workflow with a non-sensitive sample document.
  2. Submit it through a supported input method.
  3. Compare the extracted fields with the source and confirm delivery.
  4. Repeat with representative document layouts.

Company promise · paraphrase

Parseur promises to automate extraction from invoices, emails, and documents in place of manual copying, with a claim of fewer errors.

See the exact public evidence

“Stop copy-pasting. Start automating.”

Source E6 · parseur.com ↗

“AI data extractor does it automatically, with fewer errors”

Source E7 · parseur.com ↗

“turn documents into structured data.”

Source E49 · parseur.com ↗

“A parser app your ops team can run without code.”

Feature source E62 · parseur.com ↗

“connects to the tools your team already uses. No custom connectors needed.”

Feature source E59 · parseur.com ↗

Verification hypothesis · not yet tested

Does the workflow eliminate manual re-entry for the document fields the process needs?

Proposed user goal
Reduce manual copying of information from documents into operational tools.
First meaningful success
A representative document's needed data is used in a downstream task without manually retyping those fields.

What actual use would check

  1. Identify the fields currently copied from a representative document.
  2. Extract and route those fields to the downstream tool.
  3. Complete one downstream task without retyping them.
  4. Review missing or incorrect fields across additional layouts.

Still unknown

What public pages cannot establish

  • Extraction accuracy, error rates, reliability, processing speed, and performance across document types.
  • Whether delivered data matches the exact fields, formats, and downstream workflow required.
  • Specific security controls, certifications, retention practices, and fit for a data-handling requirement.
  • Pricing, limits, onboarding effort, support, and integration configuration.
  • The complete website and product scope beyond the bounded public passages used here.

Sources and evidence boundary

The preflight used bounded HTML and text from the submitted official host. It did not inspect JavaScript, images, PDFs, logins, or the product itself. Twenty of 134 captured passages were supplied to the saved analysis; the full website was not examined.
