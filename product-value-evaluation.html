Product Value Evaluation

What does the product actually prove?

We begin with the value a product publicly promises, observe what becomes visible in real first use, and document the gap without rewriting the evidence after the fact.

Commission an evaluation → Browse published evaluations →

Why PVE?

See where value holds — and where it breaks — before deciding what to change.

Product teams know what they built and what they intended users to value. PVE shows what a first-time user can actually discover, what the product can substantiate, and where those two diverge — before the team invests in changing the wrong thing.

The evaluation

A controlled look at value in use.

For teams that need a grounded view of how a product lands before deciding what to change.

01

Freeze the promise

We establish what the product says it is for, who it addresses, and what outcome it claims to create before testing begins.

02

Observe first use

The real product is used through a first-use lens defined before testing, with limited patience and without silently repairing the experience.

03

Audit the product

A broader product pass separates a first user's reaction from what the product can actually do when explored deliberately.

04

Keep evidence and judgment distinct

Public claims, observed behavior, user-lens reactions, and strategic interpretation remain traceable rather than being blended into one story.

05

State what the product proves

The result identifies where intended value becomes visible, where it disappears, and which questions deserve deeper diagnosis.

The evidence boundary

Real product interaction, not simulated opinion.

The user perspective is simulated; the product interaction is not. PVE applies a defined first-use perspective to a real product environment and observes what becomes visible, usable, or unreachable under those conditions. Evidence and interpretation remain separate and traceable.
