AI & Value Creation

The Value Is in the Path, Not the Capability

Published September 2026

TL;DR: Specialized AI products do not need to own a unique underlying capability to create meaningful value. Across the first thirty Product Value Evaluations, their value often appeared in the path around the capability: helping users begin, make the right decisions, inspect the result, and carry the task through to something usable.

A specialized AI product does not necessarily need to outperform a general-purpose model at the underlying task.

In many cases, a general-purpose AI could produce something similar. It could draft the questions for a form, propose a website structure, write a procedure, generate an image, or analyze a document.

But technical capability is only one part of the user's task.

Users may not know that the capability exists. They may not know what information to provide, how to structure the request, which steps are required, how to inspect the result, or what to do when the first output is incomplete.

A specialized product can reduce that burden through interface and workflow design. It can tell the user what to enter, what to choose, what happens next, how to inspect the result, and when the task is ready to move forward.

The product is therefore not only wrapping a model. It is packaging a capability into a usable path.

Capability Is Not the Same as a Usable Path

Comparing a specialized product with a general-purpose AI often begins with the wrong question:

Could a general-purpose model do this too?

The answer is frequently yes.

But being capable of producing an output is not the same as providing a workable route from intention to result.

A general-purpose AI can describe conditional logic. That does not automatically give the user an executable form with branches they can inspect and test.

It can propose a sitemap, write copy, and generate code. That does not automatically make those outputs part of one coherent, editable website system.

It can write instructions after being told what happened. That does not mean it observed the work, preserved the sequence, captured the relevant screens, and marked the controls that were used.

Across the PVE series, specialized value often appeared not in the isolated act of generation, but in what happened around it.

Tally: From Generated Structure to Executable Workflow

In the Tally evaluation, AI generated a substantial conditional form from a written requirement. The first result was not correct: wording, required states, options, and conditional logic still needed inspection and repair.

That imperfection did not eliminate the product's value. It revealed where the stronger value was.

The generated result existed as editable form structure rather than as prose describing what a form could contain. Its logic could be opened and corrected. Different respondent paths could be tested in Preview. The repaired form could be published, collect structured submissions, notify an owner, and route responses into Google Sheets.

A general-purpose AI could draft the questions, describe the branching logic, or generate code. Tally assembled those capabilities into an environment where the result could be inspected, repaired, executed, and connected to a real business process.

The generation was useful. The surrounding workflow made it operational.

Relume: From Separate Outputs to a Website System

The Relume evaluation showed a different form of specialized value.

A general-purpose AI can suggest a sitemap, draft page copy, propose a visual direction, and generate website code. Relume's differentiation was not that none of those things could be produced elsewhere.

It was that they existed inside one continuously editable website model.

Starting from a concise business brief rather than a supplied sitemap, Relume proposed a coherent seven-page architecture. It preserved a non-trivial service hierarchy, rendered a useful Home, and later extended the same interpreted business model across the remaining routes.

Brief, sitemap, wireframe, design, shared components, and project pages remained connected rather than becoming separate answers that the user would need to reconcile manually.

The evaluator did not have to design the website before using the website-design product.

Relume packaged several broadly available AI capabilities into a structured path from business context to an editable website system.

Scribe: Starting From the Work Itself

The Scribe evaluation demonstrated another kind of advantage.

A real browser workflow was performed once. Scribe converted it into a 16-step guide with 14 screenshots, ordered actions, and highlighted click targets.

The result still required editorial work. Much of the generated text needed rewriting, and some screenshots did not provide enough surrounding page context to orient a new reader.

But Scribe had captured work that the user would otherwise have needed to reconstruct: what happened, in what order, on which screens, and through which controls.

A general-purpose AI can write a procedure after the user describes what happened. Scribe started from the work itself and turned the observed activity into the beginning of reusable documentation.

The Product Is the Path

These products did not create entirely new AI capabilities.

They gave existing capabilities a shape.

Tally turned form generation into an editable and executable process. Relume turned website planning and generation into a connected system. Scribe turned an observed activity into the beginning of reusable documentation.

In each case, the specialized product reduced the number of decisions the user had to make before the capability became useful.

It established an entry point. It constrained the possible next steps. It preserved the state of the work. It made the result inspectable. It provided a place to correct what was incomplete and continue toward a usable outcome.

What the product packaged was not only the capability.

It packaged a procedure for using it.

A Better Question for AI Product Value

Calling a product an "AI wrapper" can obscure the part of the product that users actually experience.

The underlying model may be replaceable. The underlying capability may be widely available. A general-purpose AI may even be able to produce a similar result.

But capability alone does not tell the user where to begin, what information to provide, which decisions matter, how to inspect the result, or what to do next.

A specialized AI product can create value by turning that open-ended capability into a path the user can recognize and follow.

The better question is therefore not only:

Can a general-purpose AI perform the same underlying task?

It is:

Has this product made the capability easier to discover, direct, inspect, and carry through to a usable result?

Tally made generated logic executable and repairable.

Relume kept multiple stages of website creation inside one coherent system.

Scribe captured actions and context that the user would otherwise have to reconstruct.

Their value did not depend on owning a capability that no other AI possessed.

The value was in turning capability into procedure.

Specialized AI products do not only sell what AI can do. They sell a path for getting it done.

These observations come from using AI products directly and separating what they promise from what their real workflows make possible.
